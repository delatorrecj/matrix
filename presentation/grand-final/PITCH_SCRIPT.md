## Beat 1 — Cost of Not Knowing (0:00–0:55)

**[Slide: dark / human city — no product UI yet]**

> What if we could see the consequences of a decision  
> before a city pays the price for it?

*[Pause one beat.]*

> Today, cities make decisions about roads, evacuation routes, transport, and infrastructure  
> without being able to clearly see what those decisions will do to the people living there.

> And sometimes, the data we need simply doesn’t exist, isn’t clean, or isn’t up to date. `(LIMIT)` — pattern language, not “PH has no data”

> We cannot see the consequences.  
> And we cannot always trust the information.

*[Cascade — speak as one continuous image, not a list:]*

> A road is built. Traffic shifts. An evacuation route becomes harder to reach.  
> Flood risk moves to a barangay that wasn’t in the original plan.  
> The city discovers the cost after the concrete is already poured.

> Cities shouldn’t have to wait for reality to tell them they made the wrong decision.

## Beat 2 — What If? (0:55–1:30)

**[Slide: single question]**

> So what if a city could ask — before it builds —  
> “What happens if we put this here?”

*[Pause.]*

> Not another static study that ages the day it’s filed.  
> A way to explore possible futures — and understand who pays the price if we’re wrong.

> That is the promise. That is MATRIX.

> **MATRIX helps cities see the consequences of infrastructure decisions  
> before those consequences become reality.**

## Beat 3 — How MATRIX Works (1:30–2:15)

**[Slide: five words only]**

> It works in five steps.

1. **Simulate** — describe a project in plain language, or drop it on the map.  
2. **Visualize** — watch how the city moves when that future exists.  
3. **Compare** — five dimensions, one shared simulated reality — so the answers don’t contradict each other. `(PROVEN)`  
4. **Identify** — risks, opportunities, and where our confidence is honestly low. `(PROVEN)`  
5. **Act** — a brief a planner can actually respond to.

> We don’t pretend we already solved every data gap in Southeast Asia. `(LIMIT)`  
> MATRIX is the beginning of a larger system — the layer that lets a city see, decide, and learn what to measure next.
